## [Anthropic Releases Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-1) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Anthropic has released Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1, a point-release successor to Claude Fable 5, claiming similar or better results than Fable 5 at low or medium effort and much higher performance at higher effort tiers for coding, knowledge work, and long-running problem-solving. The company also published a system card detailing extensive internal and external risk testing conducted before release. This is a major model release from one of the leading AI labs, and the Fable/Mythos split illustrates how safety safeguards now directly shape product tiers, since both are the same underlying model differing only where cyber safeguards intervene. The exceptionally heated community debate over prose improvements, removed thought traces, and verbose output density highlights growing user sensitivity to readability and cognitive load, not just benchmark scores. Anthropic states that Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 are the same underlying model, with the gap between them reflecting tasks where earlier, less precise cyber safeguards intervened. The release notes themselves acknowledge a trade-off: while prose has fewer stock phrases and less unexplained jargon, in some cases it is denser than Fable 5's, with longer sentences and fewer paragraph breaks, and thought traces were removed in this release.

## [Cloudflare Prototypes Zstandard Cache Compression with Pingora to Save Petabytes](https://blog.cloudflare.com/cache-transcoding/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Cloudflare engineers prototyped integrating Zstandard (zstd) compression directly into their cache layer, built on their Rust-based Pingora proxy framework. The experiment explored whether compressing cached content could reclaim petabytes of storage on existing hardware without adding new machines. For a CDN operating at Cloudflare's scale, cache storage is a major infrastructure cost, and freeing petabytes through compression alone would significantly improve storage efficiency and defer hardware expansion. The approach also offers a reusable blueprint for other large-scale infrastructure operators looking to squeeze more capacity out of existing disks. Zstandard was chosen because it delivers compression ratios comparable to DEFLATE while offering much faster decompression, and its decompression speed stays roughly constant regardless of the compression level configured. Notably, this remains a prototype rather than a production deployment, and it runs on Pingora, Cloudflare's open-source proxy framework that serves over 1 trillion requests per day.

rss · The Cloudflare Blog · Sep 1, 12:59

**Background**: Zstandard (zstd) is a lossless compression algorithm developed at Meta, designed for real-time scenarios with zlib-level speed but better compression ratios, and its format is standardized in RFC8878. Pingora is Cloudflare's in-house HTTP proxy framework written in Rust, created as a memory-safe, high-performance alternative to C/C++ proxies like NGINX, and it powers Cloudflare's network at massive scale. Traditionally, CDNs apply compression such as gzip or Brotli on the wire to speed up transfers to users, whereas this experiment applies compression to content at rest in the cache itself, which is a less common optimization target.

## [Virtualizor Update Infrastructure Hijacked via BGP, Delivering Root Backdoors](https://www.virtualizor.com/blog/security-incident-bgp-hijacking/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Between August 28 and 30, 2026, attackers hijacked Virtualizor's update infrastructure through BGP route hijacking and served malicious update packages secured with valid TLS certificates. The malicious packages planted root SSH backdoor keys and Java-based persistence payloads on hypervisors; vendor Softaculous confirmed that only a small number of installations that updated during the window were affected. This is a serious supply chain attack that defeats the standard defense of TLS-verified update channels — because the malicious packages carried valid certificates, clients had no way to detect the substitution. It targets the foundational layer of hosting infrastructure (hypervisors), potentially granting root-level access to servers running thousands of customer VMs, echoing SolarWinds-style compromises of software distribution chains. Independent forensics showed the malicious package writes attacker-controlled SSH keys into root's authorized_keys, installs a Java payload, and establishes a persistence service — hosting provider AlbaHost found compromise indicators on 5 of its 34 hypervisors. Softaculous stressed that this was a hijack of the distribution chain rather than a flaw in the software's code, and stated there is no evidence that other Softaculous products were affected.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 1, 06:05

**Background**: Virtualizor is a widely used web-based VPS control panel developed by Softaculous that lets hosting providers deploy and manage virtual machines on their servers, with KVM being the most popular virtualization type. BGP hijacking is the illegitimate takeover of IP prefixes by falsely announcing ownership of them in Internet routing tables, allowing attackers to intercept traffic destined for the victim's servers — in this case, the update server itself. Because the attackers also held valid TLS certificates for the hijacked endpoints, HTTPS connections to the update server appeared legitimate, so certificate-based verification offered no protection. SSH authorized_keys backdoors are a common persistence technique in which an attacker appends their own public key to gain passwordless root access to the machine.
