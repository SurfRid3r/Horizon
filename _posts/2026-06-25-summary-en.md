## [An entire Herculaneum scroll has been read for the first time](https://scrollprize.org/firstscroll) ⭐️ 10.0/10

The Vesuvius Challenge team has successfully virtually unwrapped and read an entire Herculaneum scroll for the first time using machine learning. By applying AI models to high-resolution CT scans, they were able to detect carbon-based ink on the fragile papyrus without physically unrolling it. This breakthrough represents a major triumph for both AI and archaeology, potentially unlocking thousands of ancient texts from a library buried during the eruption of Mount Vesuvius in 79 AD. It establishes a powerful new paradigm for non-destructively discovering and preserving historical knowledge that was previously thought to be lost forever. The technique involves taking high-resolution CT scans of the carbonized scrolls, converting them into 3D voxels, and applying virtual unwrapping software to separate the flattened layers. Because the scrolls used carbon-based ink, researchers had to train neural networks to detect the subtle density differences between the ink and the carbonized papyrus, which are indistinguishable to the human eye.

hackernews · verditelabs · Jun 25, 15:48 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48675179)

**Background**: The Herculaneum papyri are over 1,800 carbonized scrolls discovered in the 18th century at the Villa of the Papyri. When Mount Vesuvius erupted in 79 AD, it buried the town under ash and rock, instantly carbonizing the scrolls and making them extremely fragile. Previous attempts to physically unroll them often destroyed the papyrus, and because they were written with carbon-based ink rather than metal-based ink, standard X-ray tomography was unable to reveal the text.

## [IBM Unveils World's First Sub-1 Nanometer Chip Technology](https://www.reuters.com/business/ibm-unveils-tech-chip-smaller-than-1-nanometer-ai-computing-push-2026-06-25/) ⭐️ 10.0/10

IBM has announced the world's first sub-1-nanometer chip technology, featuring a 0.7-nanometer transistor architecture. This new design utilizes a 3D NanoStack architecture to fit nearly 100 billion transistors into a fingernail-sized area. This breakthrough dramatically increases transistor density to twice that of IBM's 2021 2nm chips, offering up to a 50% performance boost or a 70% improvement in energy efficiency. Such advancements are paradigm-shifting for the semiconductor industry, enabling vastly more powerful and efficient computing for future AI applications. The core innovation is the 3D NanoStack architecture, which bonds two stacked transistors together vertically rather than laying them flat. While production is slated to begin within five years, IBM has not yet disclosed specific manufacturing partners for this sub-1nm technology, having previously licensed its chip tech to Samsung and Japan's Rapidus.

The basic unit of this nanostack architecture consists of two transistors stacked and bonded together, built using nanosheets. Rapidus is a Japanese semiconductor manufacturer backed by the government and major corporations like Toyota and Sony, aiming to mass-produce advanced logic semiconductors.

Anthropic sent a letter to the U.S. Senate Banking Committee on June 10, 2026, accusing Alibaba and its AI lab Qwen of conducting the largest known distillation attack against Anthropic to date. According to Anthropic, Alibaba used approximately 25,000 fraudulent accounts to generate over 28.8 million interactions with Claude models between April 22 and June 5, 2026. This allegation marks a major escalation in U.S.-China AI tensions, coming just before Congressional AI hearings and following the White House's April accusations of systematic Chinese AI IP theft. The dispute could accelerate export controls, fuel further restrictions on Chinese AI companies, and establish legal precedents for how AI model outputs are protected as intellectual property. Distillation attacks involve using a weaker model to learn from a stronger model's outputs in order to replicate its capabilities, potentially allowing the attacker to skip enormous investments in training. Anthropic warned this technique could help Chinese AI labs rapidly catch up to frontier models like Claude Mythos Preview, which has demonstrated striking cybersecurity capabilities. The U.S. Commerce Department had already restricted exports of Anthropic's Mythos and Fable models on June 12 citing national security, and Alibaba was placed on the Pentagon's 'Chinese Military Companies' list this month.

## [UN Passes World's First Global Technical Regulation for Autonomous Driving Systems](https://global.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202606/25/WS6a3cf7e7a310986e2b461ebc.html) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The UN WP.29 has passed the world's first global technical regulation for Autonomous Driving Systems (ADS GTR), co-led by China, the EU, the UK, the US, Canada, and Japan. The regulation establishes a full-lifecycle framework covering core technical specifications, manufacturer safety management, safety documentation, testing validation, and post-deployment safety. This is a critical milestone for the autonomous driving industry, as it establishes the first unified safety baseline across major automotive markets — China, the US, and the EU — reducing regulatory fragmentation and enabling more consistent global deployment. China's domestic mandatory standards already align with the ADS GTR, positioning the country to play a leading role in shaping international autonomous driving safety norms. The ADS GTR specifically targets L3 and above autonomous driving systems, where L3 requires the driver to respond to takeover requests within approximately 10 seconds, while L4 can operate independently within defined Operational Design Domains (ODD) without continuous human supervision. China contributed multiple technical proposals and testing data, and its domestic mandatory national standard further refines safety baselines and testing requirements specifically for L3 and L4 systems.

telegram · zaihuapd · Jun 25, 16:03

**Background**: The UN World Forum for Harmonization of Vehicle Regulations (WP.29) is an intergovernmental platform that develops global automotive regulations covering safety, environmental, and security standards. Prior to this regulation, there was no unified international standard for autonomous driving systems, creating compliance challenges for automakers operating across different regions. According to the SAE J3016 standard, autonomous driving is classified into six levels from L0 to L5, where L3 allows conditional automation with driver readiness to take over, and L4 achieves high automation within specific operational domains.

Cloudflare published a detailed technical blog post describing its highly performant OAuth implementation, which is built on Ory Hydra 2.x and designed to provide scalable identity and access management (IAM) for everyone. The implementation reportedly handles massive request volumes with remarkably low CPU usage, showcasing the performance capabilities of the underlying open-source framework at internet scale. This announcement demonstrates that open-source identity infrastructure like Ory can power OAuth at the scale of one of the world's largest internet infrastructure providers, validating the maturity and performance of open-source IAM solutions. It also signals Cloudflare's continued investment in identity services, which could eventually make enterprise-grade authentication and authorization more accessible to developers using its platform. The implementation is specifically built on Ory Hydra 2.x, which is the OAuth2 and OpenID Connect server component of the broader Ory identity platform. Community members note that the full Ory ecosystem also includes Kratos for identity management (login, registration, recovery, MFA), and that Cloudflare's current offering does not yet cover the complete scope of features such as user stores, SAML, and multi-tenant support.

Ory is an open-source identity and access management platform providing components for authentication, authorization, and user management, with Hydra serving as its dedicated OAuth2 and OIDC provider. Identity and Access Management (IAM) encompasses the frameworks and processes for managing digital identities and controlling user access to resources, which is critical infrastructure for any large-scale internet service.

## [SSRF in the Era of Large Models: When AI Makes Requests for You](https://xz.aliyun.com/news/92369) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The article details how attackers can weaponize Large Language Models (LLMs) via indirect prompt injection to perform Server-Side Request Forgery (SSRF) attacks. Instead of directly manipulating HTTP parameters, attackers now embed hidden prompts in web pages to trick the AI into making malicious internal or external requests. This evolution significantly expands the attack surface of traditional SSRF vulnerabilities by an order of magnitude, posing severe threats to both AI security and web infrastructure. It highlights a critical blind spot where integrating LLMs into applications can inadvertently expose previously isolated internal networks to sophisticated manipulation. The analysis provides real-world exploitation chains, a guide to setting up a reproducible vulnerable environment, and comprehensive defense strategies. It emphasizes that the attack vector has shifted from raw HTTP requests to natural language instructions that exploit the AI's ability to process untrusted data and execute web actions.

rss · 先知安全技术社区 · Jun 25, 08:09

**Background**: Server-Side Request Forgery (SSRF) is a security vulnerability where an attacker forces a server to make unauthorized requests, often bypassing network isolation to access sensitive internal systems. Indirect Prompt Injection occurs when an AI processes external data containing hidden malicious instructions, causing it to execute unintended actions. When an LLM is granted tools to fetch web content or perform actions based on retrieved data, it bridges these two vulnerabilities, allowing attackers to use natural language to "PUA" the AI into becoming a proxy for SSRF attacks.

## [Google Play Launches Third-Party Billing Options in US, UK, and EEA](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/06/play-expanded-billing.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Starting June 30, Google will roll out new Google Play billing options and fee structures in the US, UK, and EEA, allowing developers to integrate third-party in-app billing systems or use external links for purchases. The updated pricing separates the Play service fee from the Google Play Billing processing fee, setting a 10% service fee for the first $1 million in annual revenue and auto-renewing subscriptions. This represents a monumental shift in app store economics by breaking Google's billing monopoly and allowing developers to bypass traditional commission structures. It provides developers with much-needed flexibility and is a direct consequence of regulatory pressures and antitrust lawsuits. Transactions utilizing Google Play Billing in these regions will incur an additional 5% billing fee, whereas using alternative billing or external links will exempt developers from this specific charge. Developers enrolled in the "Level Up" or "Apps Experience" programs will benefit from even lower rates starting in September.

**Background**: Traditionally, major app stores like Google Play mandated the use of their proprietary billing systems, taking a significant cut of all digital sales. This practice faced immense global scrutiny, culminating in antitrust lawsuits such as the high-profile Epic Games v. Google case. In response, Google is settling these disputes and introducing alternative payment programs like the Billing Choice program to comply with regulations and lower costs for developers.
