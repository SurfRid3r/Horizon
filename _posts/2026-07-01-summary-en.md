## [Asahi Linux 7.1 Progress Report Updates Apple Silicon Linux Porting Efforts](https://asahilinux.org/2026/06/progress-report-7-1/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The Asahi Linux project has released its 7.1 progress report, detailing recent developments in porting Linux to Apple Silicon Macs with a continued focus on the M1 and M2 chip generations. The report covers ongoing work on audio subsystems (including I²S interfaces), a new AVD driver, and acknowledges the persistent challenges of keeping pace with Apple's annual hardware refresh cycle. Asahi Linux represents one of the most ambitious reverse-engineering efforts in the open-source world, bringing a native Linux experience to Apple's proprietary ARM-based processors without any official documentation or support from Apple. The project's progress directly impacts developers and enthusiasts who want to run Linux on Apple hardware, while also demonstrating the viability of community-driven hardware support against rapidly evolving proprietary platforms. The report includes technical clarifications about audio IC interfaces, noting that I²S is a distinct protocol from I²C (despite common misconceptions) and that many audio chips use separate I²C or SPI interfaces for sideband communication like volume control and clock configuration. Development remains concentrated on M1 and M2 families, as newer generations introduce additional microcontrollers and GPU changes that the small team struggles to keep up with.

hackernews · pantalaimon · Jul 1, 10:07 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48744518)

**Background**: Asahi Linux is an open-source project started by Hector Martin that aims to port the Linux kernel and related software to Apple Silicon-powered Macs through reverse engineering, as Apple provides no official hardware documentation for its SoCs. The project benefits from Apple's decision to allow booting unsigned or custom kernels on Apple Silicon Macs without a jailbreak, making the effort both legal and practical. The project's flagship distribution is Fedora Asahi Remix, a collaboration between the Asahi Linux team and the Fedora Project, while the underlying drivers and tools are intended to be upstreamed into the Linux kernel and other open-source projects.
