## [Rapid7 Discovers Actively Exploited SonicWall SMA1000 Zero-Day Vulnerabilities](https://www.rapid7.com/blog/post/etr-rapid7-mdr-team-discovers-new-sonicwall-sma1000-zero-days-being-actively-exploited-cve-2026-15409-cve-2026-15410) ⭐️ 9.0/10

On July 14, 2026, SonicWall published a security advisory addressing two vulnerabilities in SMA1000 Series remote access appliances, including the critical CVE-2026-15409 (CVSS 10.0) SSRF vulnerability and CVE-2026-15410, a high-severity code injection flaw. Rapid7's MDR team had observed active, targeted zero-day exploitation of internet-facing SMA 1000-series appliances prior to the official disclosure, and both CVEs have been added to CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog. These vulnerabilities are being actively exploited in the wild and affect widely deployed enterprise remote access appliances, making immediate patching critical for any organization using SMA1000 Series hardware. The combination of an unauthenticated SSRF with a local privilege escalation creates a powerful attack chain that can lead to full system compromise with root-level access. CVE-2026-15409 allows an unauthenticated attacker to open a websocket-based tunnel to arbitrary localhost-only services, while CVE-2026-15410 enables an attacker with access to an internal service on localhost port 8188 to execute arbitrary OS commands as root via a malicious path traversal in the remove_hotfix workflow. Affected products include SMA1000 Series models 6210, 7210, and 8200v running firmware versions 12.4.3-03245, 12.4.3-03387, 12.4.3-03434, and 12.5.0-02283.

## [EU Court Rules OpenAI Cannot Trademark 'Open AI' as It Is Descriptive](https://dpa-international.com/economics/urn:newsml:dpa.com:20090101:260715-930-389143/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The EU court has ruled against OpenAI in a trademark dispute, determining that the term 'Open AI' is purely descriptive for certain software and IT goods and services, and therefore lacks the distinctiveness required for trademark protection under EU law. This ruling prevents OpenAI from monopolizing a descriptive term that the public associates with openly accessible AI technology, which could have allowed them to sue competitors using similar language. It sets an important precedent protecting the commercial use of generic industry terms and highlights the stricter distinctiveness requirements in EU trademark law compared to the US system. The EUIPO found that 'open' would be understood as meaning freely accessible, and combined with 'AI,' it refers to products based on openly accessible artificial intelligence. Under Article 7(1)(c) of EU trademark regulations, marks that are descriptive of characteristics of goods or services cannot be registered.

hackernews · hermanzegerman · Jul 15, 14:32 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48921461)

**Background**: Under EU trademark law, a fundamental requirement for registration is distinctiveness — the ability to distinguish goods or services of one business from those of another. Article 7(1)(c) of Council Regulation (EC) No. 207/2009 specifically prohibits registering marks that designate characteristics of goods or services. Unlike the US system where trademark rights can be established through commercial use and recognition, the EU system requires names to be inherently unique and specific at the time of application, regardless of whether a product already exists in the market.

## [Red Agent POV: Single Boolean Breaks B2B Credit System](https://www.wiz.io/blog/red-agent-pov-business-logic) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A security researcher successfully bypassed a B2B platform's credit and paywall system simply by changing a single client-side boolean value from 'false' to 'true'. This discovery highlights a critical business logic vulnerability where financial constraints were completely circumvented through trivial client-side manipulation. This type of vulnerability allows malicious actors to abuse premium features or resources indefinitely, potentially causing significant financial losses for the affected business. It serves as a crucial reminder that applications must never trust client-side state for enforcing server-side rules and must implement strict backend validation. The root cause is a classic response manipulation flaw where the application trusted client-visible state to enforce server-side rules. An attacker only needs a simple intercepting proxy or browser developer tools to modify the boolean flag, which indicates that the backend system lacked proper authorization checks.

rss · Wiz Blog | RSS feed · Jul 15, 13:33

**Background**: Business logic vulnerabilities exploit flaws in an application's intended workflow, often allowing attackers to misuse valid functionality without triggering typical security alarms. Relying entirely on client-side data to enforce critical constraints like user permissions, pricing, or credit limits is fundamentally insecure, as motivated users can easily manipulate responses in their browser. Security experts strongly recommend implementing zero-trust architecture principles and validating all critical business rules on the server side.
