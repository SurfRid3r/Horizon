## [OpenAI's Claimed AI-Generated Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize Proof Sparks Controversy](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

OpenAI announced an AI-generated solution to the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness Millennium Prize Problem, claiming an internal system produced a proof that the dynamics of the fluid-motion Navier–Stokes equations can develop a singularity in finite time, released alongside both a writeup and a Lean formalization. The claim immediately ignited controversy after NYU mathematician Tristan Buckmaster posted a statement suggesting the work may have built on external researchers' prior work and prompts, and Terence Tao publicly commented as well. A verified AI-generated solution to a Millennium Prize Problem would be paradigm-shifting for both mathematics and AI research, and could claim the Clay Mathematics Institute's $1 million award. Beyond the mathematics itself, the disputed provenance of the result makes this a landmark case for AI research ethics and attribution in AI-assisted scientific discovery. The proof claims to demonstrate finite-time blow-up (singularity formation) for the Navier–Stokes equations, and OpenAI released both a paper writeup and a formalization in the Lean proof assistant. Separately, OpenAI reportedly claims the internal model, trained for less than two weeks, is more than twice as capable in mathematics as Astra, which was made public only a week earlier, while the prior work allegedly underpinning the result came from mathematicians not affiliated with OpenAI.

hackernews · OpenAI Research News · Sep 8, 17:13 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49613262)

**Background**: The Navier–Stokes equations, formulated in the 19th century, describe fluid motion, and the Clay Mathematics Institute's existence and smoothness problem asks whether smooth solutions in three dimensions always exist or can develop singularities (blow-up) in finite time. In 2000, the Clay Institute designated seven Millennium Prize Problems, each carrying a $1 million award, and only the Poincaré conjecture has been solved so far. Progress on Navier–Stokes is considered a first step toward understanding turbulence, one of the great unsolved phenomena in physics. Lean is a proof assistant that allows mathematical proofs to be checked with full logical rigor by computer.

## [Mathematicians Claim Navier-Stokes Blowup Progress, Accuse OpenAI of Research Theft](https://cims.nyu.edu/~tristanb/statement.pdf) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Mathematicians Tristan Buckmaster and Levent Alpöge announced progress on August 15 on finite-time blowup with smooth forcing for incompressible porous media equations, the Boussinesq system, and the 3D incompressible Euler equations — related to, but not a full solution of, the $1 million Clay Millennium Navier-Stokes problem. Simultaneously, Buckmaster published a statement accusing OpenAI of accessing their unpublished work through user data and attempting to intimidate them into silence, shortly after OpenAI announced its own model-generated proof of Navier-Stokes finite-time blowup (statements C and D of Fefferman's formulation). This is a dual shockwave: genuine mathematical progress on a Millennium Prize problem that had resisted attack for decades, combined with unprecedented allegations that a leading AI lab harvested researchers' private chat data and used corporate pressure to suppress their publication. If substantiated, the accusations could fundamentally undermine researchers' trust in using AI assistants for sensitive work and reshape norms around data privacy and academic priority in the AI era. According to the researchers' statement, OpenAI offered to publicly credit them as deserving the Clay Prize and being the "closest humans to the problem," and when Buckmaster threatened to go public, a representative asked "Why would you ruin your career?" OpenAI's own proof remains unverified by the mathematical community, the company has said it will decline the Clay prize, and OpenAI researcher Sebastian Bubeck has publicly denied the allegations on Twitter.

hackernews · procedurecall · Sep 8, 05:42 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49605915)

**Background**: The Navier-Stokes equations, developed in the 19th century by Navier and Stokes, describe the motion of viscous fluids and underpin the modeling of aircraft, blood flow, and pollution. A central open question is whether smooth three-dimensional solutions always remain smooth or can develop singularities ("blow up") in finite time — this is one of the seven Clay Mathematics Institute Millennium Prize Problems, carrying a $1 million prize. Buckmaster and Alpöge's results concern blowup for closely related systems (porous media, Boussinesq, and Euler equations), which experts view as a key stepping stone toward the full Navier-Stokes problem. Notably, Alpöge works at Anthropic, a direct competitor of OpenAI, adding another layer of corporate rivalry to the priority dispute.

The program applies predictive policing to ordinary Americans' domestic travel based on financial behavior, inverting the traditional model in which investigations begin with suspicion of an actual crime. ## [Google Project Zero: Testing Race Conditions via Memory Tracing and Delay Injection](https://projectzero.google/2026/09/maccconc-race-condition.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Google Project Zero researcher Jann Horn published a new technique for reliably triggering race conditions in tests by combining memory access tracing with stack-based delay injection. The approach targets three chronic pain points he identifies: confirming bug candidates found manually or via static analysis, writing regression tests that reliably reproduce a fixed race condition bug, and letting fuzzers reach code paths that only execute when operations are racing. Race conditions are a common class of security bug, yet they are notoriously hard to reproduce because the negative effect only appears when threads interleave in exactly the right way. A method that can force such interleavings on demand would make vulnerability confirmation far more reliable, keep fixed concurrency bugs from silently regressing in test suites, and extend automated fuzzing into a bug class that current fuzzers largely fail to explore. The core idea is to instrument memory accesses and then inject delays keyed off the call stack, steering thread scheduling toward the desired racy interleaving instead of relying on repeated runs and luck. Because the technique deliberately perturbs execution timing, it is aimed at controlled test scenarios — bug confirmation, regression suites, and fuzzing — where deterministic triggering of a specific interleaving is the goal.

rss · Project Zero · Sep 8, 07:00

**Background**: A race condition arises in multithreaded programs when multiple threads access shared data and the outcome depends on the exact order in which the scheduler interleaves their steps, so buggy behavior may surface only rarely or under specific timing. Memory access tracing is an established technique that executes extra instrumentation code on every memory access of a program, long used for debugging, taint checking, and dataflow tracking. Fuzzing is automated bug discovery that bombards software with malformed or random inputs to trigger crashes, but covering the interesting interleavings of concurrent operations is far harder for fuzzers than covering sequential code paths. Jann Horn is a highly regarded security researcher at Google Project Zero, known for numerous high-profile vulnerability discoveries.

## [Google GTIG: Adversarial AI Evolves from Prompting to Autonomous Agentic Attacks](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/from-prompting-to-autonomy-the-evolution-of-adversarial-ai/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Google's Threat Intelligence Group (GTIG) reports that adversaries have progressed from basic AI prompting to autonomous agentic AI workflows that dramatically reduce human-in-the-loop latency. In Q2 2026, GTIG observed threat actors compromise a cloud resource and plan, build, and execute an agent-enabled mass credential harvesting campaign in under six hours, while also tracking UNC6780 tricking AI coding assistants and LLM security scanners into open source software supply chain compromises. This evolution compresses the traditional response window available to defenders, forcing security teams to contend with machine-speed attacks rather than human-paced operations. It also signals that enterprise AI assets—from model weights and source code to API credentials and cloud compute quotas—have become high-value targets for espionage, extortion, and resource theft. The report identifies five key Q2 2026 trends: expanding software supply chain risks (targeting developers, AI coding assistants, and LLM security scanners), theft of proprietary AI models, code, prompts, and research, agentic multi-agent frameworks that autonomously manage scanning pipelines and resolve operational errors, AI as a force multiplier across the full attack lifecycle including scaled information operations, and LLMjacking via stolen developer credentials, purchased compromised accounts, and hijacked cloud infrastructure. The findings are grounded in telemetry from frontline Mandiant incident response engagements.

rss · Google Threat Intelligence · Sep 8, 14:00

**Background**: GTIG is Google's threat intelligence team that publishes recurring trackers on adversarial AI use, building on telemetry from Mandiant, the incident response firm Google acquired in 2022. "Agentic AI" refers to AI systems that can autonomously plan, chain together tools, and execute multi-step tasks with minimal human input, unlike prompt-based interactions where a human directs each step. Credential harvesting is the mass collection of valid user credentials, which adversaries then use or sell for unauthorized access. "LLMjacking" describes the theft or abuse of LLM API credentials and accounts to run unauthorized high-performance AI workloads at the victim's expense.

## [OpenAI Launches ChatGPT Images 2.0 with Reasoning, Web Search, and Better Text Rendering](https://t.me/zaihuapd/43693) ⭐️ 8.0/10

OpenAI has released ChatGPT Images 2.0, a new image generation experience built on the GPT Image 2 model, which introduces a thinking (reasoning) capability and web search into the image generation pipeline. The model can produce up to 8 visually consistent sequential images from a single prompt at up to 2K resolution, and significantly improves text rendering for non-Latin scripts such as Chinese, Japanese, and Korean. Accurate text rendering—especially for non-Latin scripts—has been a long-standing weakness of AI image generators, so this release directly addresses a pain point that limits practical use in design, comics, and marketing across East Asian markets. Combining reasoning and web search with image generation also raises the competitive bar, enabling more complex, better-informed compositions such as UI mockups, multi-panel comics, and marketing materials. Beyond the headline features, GPT Image 2 is described as a quality-first, reasoning-driven model that plans composition before generating, supports multi-reference image-to-image editing with up to 16 input images, and can output up to 4K. Notably, the 2K output cap reported for ChatGPT Images 2.0 is lower than the 4K maximum cited for the underlying model.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 8, 18:45

**Background**: Text rendering in AI image generation refers to a model's ability to accurately draw legible, correctly-spelled text as part of a generated image—historically difficult because diffusion models generate pixels holistically rather than forming characters stroke by stroke. Previous models like GPT Image 1.5 already ranked near the top of text-rendering benchmarks (scoring 4.88/5.00), yet scripts with thousands of characters, such as Chinese and Japanese, remained especially error-prone. Maintaining visual consistency across multiple generated images (e.g., the same character appearing across comic panels) is another classic challenge for generative models, which GPT Image 2's reasoning-driven approach aims to solve.
