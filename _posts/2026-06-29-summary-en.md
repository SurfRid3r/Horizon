## [US Supreme Court Rules Geofence Warrants Trigger Fourth Amendment Protections](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jun/29/supreme-court-geofence-warrants-case-decision) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The US Supreme Court has officially ruled that law enforcement's use of highly intrusive geofence warrants must be subject to Fourth Amendment constitutional protections. The decision restricts the ability of police to conduct dragnet-style data collection from technology companies without proper constitutional safeguards. This landmark decision profoundly impacts digital privacy and mass surveillance, establishing a crucial legal paradigm shift to protect citizens from overly intrusive digital tracking. It also forces tech companies to re-evaluate their data retention practices to ensure better privacy protections for their users. In the specific case of Chatrie, Google provided law enforcement with data of devices located within 150 meters of a bank robbery over a one-hour window. Google has since shifted its Location History feature to device-side storage instead of centralizing it, specifically to minimize compliance with these broad warrants.

hackernews · cdrnsf · Jun 29, 15:54 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48720924)

**Background**: A geofence warrant (or reverse location warrant) allows law enforcement to request data from tech companies, such as Google's Sensorvault, to identify all active mobile devices within a specific geographic area during a set time period. Unlike traditional warrants that require probable cause for a specific suspect, geofence warrants operate as general searches, often capturing data from innocent bystanders. The Fourth Amendment protects citizens against unreasonable searches and seizures, requiring law enforcement to obtain warrants based on probable cause.

## [HackerRank's Open-Source ATS Shows LLM Resume Scoring Is Highly Inconsistent](https://danunparsed.com/p/hackerrank-open-source-ats) ⭐️ 8.0/10

## [Miasma Worm Attack Disables 73 Microsoft GitHub Repositories in 105 Seconds](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s?__biz=MzkyMTI0NjA3OA==&mid=2247495259&idx=1&sn=adebb88353849918e5d284d45187fc02) ⭐️ 8.0/10

On June 5, 2026, a supply chain attack known as the Miasma worm automatically disabled 73 Microsoft GitHub repositories in just 105 seconds after a developer opened a polluted project folder in VS Code. The attack toolset publicly targets AI coding tools by injecting via SessionStart hooks and utilizing GitHub commit searches for command-and-control (C2) operations. This event highlights a severe vulnerability in the developer supply chain, demonstrating how rapidly local compromises can escalate to mass repository takedowns. It exposes critical security risks in widely used developer environments and AI coding tools, threatening the integrity of global software development pipelines. The Miasma worm specifically targets 13 AI tools, including Claude Code and Copilot, operating with zero CVEs and forging SLSA provenance to bypass security. It establishes its command-and-control infrastructure by using three independent GitHub commit search channels, each with a distinct search string and purpose.

rss · M01NTeam · Jun 29, 10:00

**Background**: A supply chain attack targets the weakest links in a software development pipeline, often compromising trusted components or environments to spread malicious code. SessionStart hooks are automated scripts that execute when an application session begins, frequently used in tools like VS Code to customize workflows but exploitable for arbitrary code execution. SLSA (Supply-chain Levels for Software Artifacts) is a framework intended to ensure the integrity of software artifacts, though attackers can forge its provenance to appear legitimate.
