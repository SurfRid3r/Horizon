Varroa mites are arguably the single most destructive pest threatening honeybee colonies worldwide, contributing significantly to colony collapse and economic losses in the beekeeping industry. A targeted biological treatment could revolutionize pest management by reducing reliance on existing miticides, which can render honey inedible and face growing mite resistance. ## [Cloudflare Urges Immediate Adoption of ML-DSA for Post-Quantum Signatures](https://blog.cloudflare.com/ml-dsa-will-have-to-do/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Cloudflare published a detailed analysis of the nine new post-quantum signature algorithms currently being evaluated by NIST, arguing that the industry should deploy ML-DSA now rather than waiting for these future candidates. ML-DSA was already standardized by NIST as FIPS 204 in August 2024, making it the most mature and production-ready option available today. The transition to post-quantum cryptography is urgent due to the "harvest now, decrypt later" threat, where adversaries can store encrypted data now and decrypt it once quantum computers mature. Cloudflare's recommendation provides security practitioners with a clear, actionable path rather than decision paralysis while waiting for potentially better algorithms years down the line. ML-DSA is based on the CRYSTALS-Dilithium lattice-based signature scheme and is one of the three standards NIST finalized as FIPS 204 on August 13, 2024. The nine new candidates under evaluation represent alternative approaches (such as hash-based, code-based, and multivariate schemes) that could provide diversity beyond lattice-based cryptography, but they are still years away from standardization.

rss · The Cloudflare Blog · Jul 9, 14:00

**Background**: NIST's Post-Quantum Cryptography Standardization program began in 2016 to develop cryptographic standards resistant to attacks by quantum computers, which could break widely-used public-key algorithms like RSA and ECC. After multiple rounds of evaluation, NIST released the first three final PQC standards in August 2024: FIPS 203 (ML-KEM for key establishment), FIPS 204 (ML-DSA for signatures), and FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA for signatures). NIST is now evaluating additional signature candidates to broaden the portfolio of standardized post-quantum algorithms.
